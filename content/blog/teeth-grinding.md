If you wake up with a sore jaw, a dull headache at your temples, or teeth that feel
slightly loose, there's a good chance you're clenching or grinding in your sleep. Dentists
call it bruxism, and we see signs of it in roughly one in three adults who come in.

## How we can tell

Most people who grind have no idea they do it. In the chair, the clues are hard to miss:

- Flat, shiny spots on the tips of the canines and the chewing surfaces of molars
- Small notches at the gumline, where enamel has flexed and chipped away
- Scalloped edges along the sides of the tongue
- Tight, tender chewing muscles when we press just in front of the ears

## Why it happens

Stress is the usual suspect, and it is a real factor. But grinding is also linked to
caffeine late in the day, alcohol close to bedtime, some antidepressants, and sleep-disordered
breathing. If you snore or wake up unrefreshed, mention it; grinding is sometimes the body's
way of keeping the airway open.

## What a night guard does (and doesn't do)

A custom night guard won't stop you from clenching, but it moves the force onto a
replaceable piece of hard acrylic instead of your enamel. Most patients also notice that
their jaw muscles relax within a few weeks.

Drugstore boil-and-bite guards are soft and thick. Some people chew on them, which can make
muscle soreness worse. A custom guard is thin, hard, and shaped to your bite so your jaw can
rest in a neutral position. You can read more about how we make ours on our
[night guard page](/services/night-guards/).

## Things to try tonight

- Stop caffeine by early afternoon.
- Notice your jaw during the day. Teeth should be apart, lips together, tongue resting on
  the roof of your mouth.
- Put a warm washcloth on your jaw muscles for ten minutes before bed.

If the soreness lasts more than a couple of weeks, or your jaw clicks or locks, come see us.
